Inside a Real Logistics Audit: 6 Critical Flags, 12 Rate Anomalies, and $79,651 in Exposure

A breakdown of what our audit engine found when we ran it against a real multi-year freight statement.

Why manual reconciliation breaks down

For many freight forwarders and logistics teams, reconciling carrier statements and invoices is a manual, spreadsheet-driven ritual. It scales poorly: a single multi-year account can contain thousands of rows, dozens of formats, and a host of human errors. Small mismatches, such as a mistyped invoice number, a shifted date column from an Excel export, or an inconsistent rate notation, slip past spot checks and quietly accumulate into material discrepancies.

What we did

To understand the failure modes, the audit engine was run against a real multi-year freight statement spanning 2020–2024. The dataset included hundreds of invoice line items across multiple carriers, with mixed Excel and CSV sources, legacy Excel-formatted exports, and manual journal entries. The goal was to surface mismatches, anomalous rates, and any silently corrupted fields that a human reviewer would likely miss.

What it found

  • 6 critical-severity flags found, including genuine ghost payments referencing missing invoices, plus related balance and ledger mismatches.
  • 12 high-severity rate anomalies flagged per container by comparing each line against the account's median rates, with a number of shipments showing rates 40–60% higher than the recent median for similar routes and container sizes.
  • $79,651 in total estimated financial exposure identified across 224 real statement rows.
  • Formatting issues, including Excel serial date bugs that had silently corrupted date fields during CSV round-trips, producing shifted dates or epoch-like numeric values in some rows.

Why this matters

Each of these findings on its own might look small. Together they illustrate how errors compound over time. A missing invoice reference prevents automated matching and can mask duplicate payments; rate anomalies can inflate costs if unchecked; formatting bugs can break date-based accruals and aging reports. For accounts maintained by hand, these issues are effectively invisible until someone notices a material break in cash flow or an audit triggers a deep reconciliation.

Closing

Modern audit tooling that normalizes formats, validates references, and flags statistical outliers turns a guessing game into a repeatable process. For teams that run long-lived accounts, a periodic automated audit dramatically reduces risk and frees finance teams to act on exceptions rather than hunting for them.
